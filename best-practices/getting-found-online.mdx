Having a website is just the first step — it's your home base online, but customers still need a way to discover it. This guide covers everything else that drives people to find and choose your business: from Google and local directories to social media and word of mouth. Work through these steps and you'll have one of the strongest online presences of any local business in your area.

## Your Online Presence Checklist

Use this checklist to track where you stand. Aim to complete every item — each one adds another way for customers to find you and signals to Google that your business is real, active, and trustworthy.

- ✅ **Website live and published** — Your LocalSite site is live and clearly displays your business name, address, phone number, and hours. Don't make customers hunt for the basics.

- ✅ **Google Business Profile claimed and complete** — Log in at [business.google.com](https://business.google.com) and make sure your photos, hours, description, and business categories are all filled in. An incomplete profile ranks lower than a complete one.

- ✅ **Yelp listing claimed** — Yelp is especially important for restaurants, salons, and any consumer-facing service. Claim your free listing and add photos, hours, and a business description.

- ✅ **Facebook Business Page created** — Even a basic Facebook page with your address and website link helps Google verify that your business exists. It also gives customers another way to find you and leave recommendations.

- ✅ **NAP is consistent** — Your Name, Address, and Phone number (NAP) must match exactly across your website, Google, Yelp, Facebook, and every other directory. Inconsistencies confuse Google and can lower your local ranking.

- ✅ **At least 5 Google reviews** — Reviews are a major local ranking factor. Reach out to a handful of recent happy customers and ask them to leave a quick review. A direct link makes it easy for them.

- ✅ **Site is mobile-friendly** — More than half of local searches happen on phones. Use LocalSite's mobile preview (in the editor toolbar) to confirm your site looks great on a small screen before you publish.

## Getting Your First Customers Online

Once your online presence is set up, get the word out. You don't need an advertising budget to generate your first wave of online traffic — a little promotion goes a long way.

- **Share your new website on your personal social media.** Post your website link on your personal Facebook or Instagram page and tell your network you're open for business. Ask friends and family to share it — early word-of-mouth is free and surprisingly effective.
- **Add your website URL to your email signature.** Every email you send becomes a passive advertisement for your business. It takes two minutes to set up and works around the clock.
- **Print your URL everywhere customers might see it.** Business cards, receipts, invoices, menus, store signage — anywhere your name appears, your website address should too.
- **Ask existing customers to share your site.** A loyal customer who recommends your site to one friend is worth more than a paid ad. Don't be shy about asking — most happy customers are glad to help.

## Paid Advertising (When You're Ready)

Once you've completed the free steps above, paid advertising can accelerate your visibility significantly. Two options worth knowing about:

- **Google Local Services Ads** — These ads appear at the very top of Google search results for local service searches. You pay per lead, not per click, and Google displays a "Google Guaranteed" badge that builds instant trust with customers.
- **Facebook and Instagram Ads** — Meta's ad platform lets you target people within a specific radius of your business. It's one of the most cost-effective ways to reach potential customers in your neighborhood who may not be actively searching yet.

There's no rush to start paid ads — the checklist above delivers strong results for free. But when you're ready to grow faster, these are the channels local businesses see the best results from.

<Note>
  Building online visibility takes time. Focus on the checklist above first — these free actions have the highest return on investment for a local business. Paid ads and advanced tactics work best once your foundation is solid.
</Note>
