Every great business website is made up of pages that guide visitors to the information they need most. LocalSite makes it easy to build exactly the page structure that works for your business — add a gallery to show off your work, a services page to list your offerings, or a blog to share news and updates. You control which pages appear in your navigation menu and in what order, so your visitors always have a clear path forward.

## Adding a New Page

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open your site editor">
    Sign in to LocalSite and click **Edit Site** from your dashboard to open the visual editor.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Open the Pages panel">
    Click **Pages** in the left panel to see a list of all the pages currently on your site.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Click Add Page">
    At the bottom of the Pages panel, click the **+ Add Page** button to start the page creation flow.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose a page type">
    Select the type of page you want to create. Your options include:
    - **Blank** — Start from scratch with an empty canvas.
    - **About Us** — A pre-built layout for your story and team.
    - **Services** — A structured page for listing what you offer.
    - **Gallery** — A photo-first layout for showcasing your work.
    - **Menu** — Ideal for restaurants and cafés to list food and drink items.
    - **Contact** — Includes your address, phone, a contact form, and a map.
    - **Blog** — A feed of posts for sharing news, tips, and updates.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Name your page">
    Type a name for the page in the field provided. This name becomes the label that appears in your site's navigation menu, so keep it short and clear (for example, "Our Work" or "Book Now").
  </Step>
  <Step title="Click Add to create the page">
    Click **Add** and your new page will appear in the Pages panel and in the site's navigation menu, ready for you to edit.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Common Page Types

Not sure which pages your site needs? Here are the most popular pages local businesses add and what each one is for:

- **Home** — Your main page, always shown first. It's your business's front door and should give visitors a strong first impression with your key message and a clear call to action.
- **About Us** — Tell your story and introduce your team. This page builds trust and helps customers feel connected to the people behind your business.
- **Services / Menu** — List what you offer, along with prices where relevant. Make it easy for potential customers to understand exactly how you can help them.
- **Gallery** — Showcase your work, your space, or your products with photos. Visual proof is one of the most powerful tools for winning new customers.
- **Contact** — Display your address, phone number, a contact form, and an embedded map. Make it as easy as possible for customers to reach you or find you.

## Reordering Your Menu

The order of your pages in the navigation menu matters — it shapes how visitors explore your site. To change the order, open the **Pages** panel in the editor and drag any page up or down in the list using the handle icon (⠿) on the left side of each page row. Your navigation menu updates instantly to match the new order. Put your most important pages — like Services or Book Now — near the top so they're always easy to find.

## Hiding or Deleting Pages

Sometimes you need to temporarily remove a page from your site, or remove it entirely. LocalSite gives you two options:

- **Hide a page** — Click the eye icon (👁) next to the page name in the Pages panel to toggle it off. The page stays in your editor with all its content intact, but it disappears from your navigation menu and is no longer accessible to visitors. Toggle it back on at any time.
- **Delete a page** — Click the three-dot menu (⋯) next to a page name and select **Delete**. This permanently removes the page and all of its content.

<Warning>
  Deleted pages cannot be recovered. If there's any chance you'll want the page back in the future, use the **Hide** option instead — it removes the page from your live site without losing any of your work.
</Warning>
