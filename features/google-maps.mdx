For any business with a physical location — a restaurant, salon, retail shop, or service area — a map on your website is essential. Many of your customers are finding you on their phone while they're already out, and they need to know exactly where you are and how to get there without any friction. An embedded Google Map lets them see your location at a glance, get turn-by-turn directions with one tap, and feel confident they can find you. It's a small addition that can make a real difference in how many people actually walk through your door.

## Adding a Map to Your Site

<Steps>
  <Step title="Go to the page where you want the map">
    In your editor, navigate to your **Contact page** — or any page where you'd like the map to appear.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add a Map section">
    Click **"+"** to add a new section and choose **"Map"** from the section menu.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Confirm your address auto-fills">
    LocalSite automatically fills in the map using the address from your **Business Info** settings. Your location should appear on the map immediately.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add your address if needed">
    If your address hasn't been added yet, the map will prompt you. Click **"Update Business Info"** to add your street address, city, and ZIP code, then return to the editor.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save the page">
    Click **Save** — a live, interactive Google Map now appears on your page. Visitors can zoom, pan, and tap for directions right from your website.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  The map is interactive — visitors can zoom in and out, pan around, and tap **"Get Directions"** to open Google Maps or Apple Maps on their phone with your address pre-loaded as the destination.
</Note>

## Customizing the Map Section

Once your map is on the page, you can adjust how it looks using the settings panel on the right side of the editor:

- **Height** — Choose from **Small**, **Medium**, or **Large** to control how tall the map section appears on your page. Medium is a good default for most Contact pages.
- **"Get Directions" button** — Toggle this option on to display a clearly labeled button directly below the map. On mobile, tapping it opens the customer's preferred maps app with your address ready to go.

## Linking to Your Google Business Profile

Embedding a map on your website is a great first step — but to get the most out of local search, you should also claim your **Google Business Profile** (formerly called Google My Business).

Your Google Business Profile is the panel that appears on the right side of Google search results when someone searches your business name. It shows your address, hours, phone number, photos, and reviews — and it often appears *above* regular search results for local queries.

To get started, visit [business.google.com](https://business.google.com) and claim or create your listing.

<Note>
  Google Business Profile is a separate, free product from Google — it's not part of LocalSite. However, keeping the address, phone number, and hours on your Google Business Profile consistent with what's on your LocalSite website helps with local search rankings.
</Note>

<Tip>
  Place the map near your address and phone number on your Contact page — customers often look for all three at once when deciding whether to visit. Grouping them together makes it easy and removes any hesitation.
</Tip>
