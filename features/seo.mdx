SEO — short for Search Engine Optimization — is the practice of making your website easier for Google to understand so it shows your business to people who are actively searching for what you offer. You don't need to be a developer or a marketing expert to get results. For a local business, SEO mostly comes down to a few simple things: making sure Google knows your business name, what you do, and where you're located. LocalSite gives you the tools to set this up in minutes, right from your dashboard.

## Your SEO Settings Panel

LocalSite gives you two ways to access your SEO settings:

- **Site-wide settings** — Go to **Dashboard → SEO** to set defaults that apply to your entire website. This is the best place to start.
- **Per-page settings** — In the editor, click the **Settings** icon (gear icon) for any individual page to set SEO fields specific to that page. Per-page settings override the site-wide defaults.

Most local businesses only need to worry about the site-wide settings and their home page. Start there before fine-tuning other pages.

## Key SEO Fields

<CardGroup cols={1}>
  <Card title="Page Title">
    The headline Google shows in search results when your site appears. It's also displayed in the browser tab. Use your business name and a keyword that describes what you do and where — for example, *"Mario's Pizza | Best Pizza in Austin, TX"*. Keep it under 60 characters so it doesn't get cut off.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Meta Description">
    A 1–2 sentence summary that appears under your page title in Google search results. Think of it as a short advertisement for your business. Write something that would make a potential customer want to click — mention your service, your location, and what makes you stand out. Aim for 140–160 characters.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Keywords">
    An optional comma-separated list of terms your customers might type into Google — for example, *"pizza delivery Austin, wood-fired pizza, family restaurant Austin TX"*. Keywords are less influential than they used to be, but they help LocalSite understand your business and can support other SEO signals.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Business Schema">
    You don't need to do anything here — LocalSite automatically adds structured data (called "schema markup") to your website behind the scenes. This tells Google your business name, address, hours, and other details in a format it can read directly. When set up correctly, Google can display this information in search results and on Google Maps.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

## SEO Tips for Local Businesses

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Use your city name in your page title">
    Local customers search with location in mind — they type things like *"best plumber in Denver"* or *"hair salon near me."* Including your city (or neighborhood) in your page title helps Google match your site to those searches. For example: *"Denver's Trusted Plumber | Quick Fix Plumbing"* is far more effective than just *"Quick Fix Plumbing."*
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Write a meta description that mentions your service and location">
    Your meta description won't directly boost your ranking, but it influences whether someone clicks on your listing. A description like *"Mario's Pizza has served wood-fired pies to Austin families since 2005. Order online or visit us on South Congress Ave."* tells the reader exactly what to expect and builds trust before they even visit your site.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Keep your address consistent everywhere">
    Google cross-references your business information across many sources — your website, Google Business Profile, Yelp, Facebook, and local directories. If your address appears differently in different places (e.g. *"123 Main St"* on one site and *"123 Main Street, Suite 1"* on another), it creates confusion and can hurt your rankings. Use the exact same name, address, and phone number format everywhere your business is listed.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Ask happy customers to leave Google reviews">
    Reviews are one of the strongest signals Google uses for local rankings. A business with 50 genuine four- and five-star reviews will almost always outrank a competitor with none. After a great job or visit, don't be shy — send a follow-up message asking your customer to share their experience on Google. You can find your direct review link in your Google Business Profile dashboard.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

<Tip>
  The single best thing you can do for local SEO is claim and complete your **Google Business Profile** at [business.google.com](https://business.google.com). It's free, takes about 15 minutes to set up, and often shows up above regular search results when someone searches for your type of business nearby. If you haven't done this yet, make it your first priority.
</Tip>
